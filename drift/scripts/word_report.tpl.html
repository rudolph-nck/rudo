Four director's calls

Weight vs drift

Every effect sits on one axis: heavy (stone, press, carve) or weightless (drift, float, stars). Each section's place on that axis comes from its energy and its words.

DIE is never an effect

The song says "I don't wanna die" five times and means it. DIE stays a small, still caption every time. The hero of that couplet is always DRIFT.

NO is an eraser

Each NO runs the hot point backwards and unmakes something we've already seen. The breakdown erases faster and faster until only SILENCE is left.

The silence at the end

After "Let me drift" there are 11 seconds of near-silence (just a breath at 4:02). Then the beat comes back at 4:09. That's tomorrow: one hot point redraws him and the title.

How the timing was measured

This environment can't reach the speech-recognition and vocal-separation models we used for Shout It to the Sky (GitHub releases, Hugging Face and the model hosts are blocked). So the timing comes from three things: the lines Suno highlights in your export video (read frame by frame), a voice-isolation filter, and my hand-check of the spectrogram at every chorus, the intro and the outro. Line starts are good to about ±0.2 s. Words inside a line marked "est." are spread by syllables, and I hand-sync them per section during the build.

One thing would make this exact: the vocal stem from Suno (Get Stems → Vocals, as WAV). With it I can measure every word and held note directly.
